### What It's About
Narrated by Nick Carraway, the novel chronicles the enigmatic life of Jay Gatsby, a self-made millionaire who buys a sprawling mansion in West Egg across the bay from his former love, Daisy Buchanan. Gatsby stages lavish, bacchanalian parties in the desperate hope of reclaiming a lost romance that has long since calcified into an unattainable ideal.

### What Resonated Most
Throughout The Great Gatsby, Francis Scott Fitzgerald persistently employs the potent and multifaceted color symbolism of the green light to emphasize the illusory nature of the American Dream. Not only does the green light represent the unattainable nature of the American Dream, but also the dissatisfaction with it, reflecting Gatsby's shifting circumstances. It correlates to Gatsby's desire and relentless pursuit of an idealized past - an unachievable attempt to recover an idea of himself that had gone into loving Daisy.

### Reflections on the Broader Message
In chapter seven, the figurative bullet that kills Gatsby is Daisy's hesitation and uncertainty in confessing her love for him. Long before Wilson's physical bullet arrives at the pool, Gatsby's vision collapses under the weight of reality. Fitzgerald portrays West Egg's raw vigor against East Egg's careless aristocratic cruelty: Tom and Daisy smash up things and creatures and then retreat back into their money, leaving others to clean up the wreckage of their illusions.

01 · May 2025 · 9.5/10

Frankenstein

by Mary Shelley

“Similar ambitions can diverge at the precipice of ethical awareness, distinguishing pursuits led by compassionate moderation from those corrupted by destructive isolation.”

— me, May 2025
The Enlightenment's rationalist exaltation of scientific triumphalism, characterized by a rampant objectification of nature and a pathological rejection of the metaphysical, imposed a dogmatic intellectual hegemony that systematically alienated the profound depths of human vulnerability. Shelley employs the foils of Victor Frankenstein and Henry Clerval to highlight the dichotomy between an unrestrained ambition, one that undermines humanity, and an ethically rooted pursuit, one that harmonizes with it.

02 · Feb 2024 · 9.5/10

Advice to Youth

by Mark Twain

“The unquestioning, naive acceptance of advice from authority figures promotes conformity over genuine individual growth, impeding the creation of a unique perspective paramount for adaptation and innovation.”

— me, Feb 2024
For generations, society has exalted the virtues of elders, often adhering to their advice without question and idolizing them as infallible. Twain challenges this traditional notion, cautioning youth against exclusively relying on older counsel, suggesting that those who do will be surprised at how sharply their character resembles everyone else's. The naive acceptance of advice from authority figures promotes conformity over genuine individual growth, impeding the creation of an independent perspective on life. With biting satire, Twain ridicules cliches about unyielding obedience, showing that real-world situations require nuance, common sense, and personal judgment.

03 · Dec 2023 · 9.5/10

Self-Reliance & Nature

by Ralph Waldo Emerson

“Most of the problems we encounter are not caused by external forces, but derived from our own self-doubt; we cast shadows over our talents, hampering our drive toward success.”

— me, Dec 2023
In the early 19th century, transcendentalism emerged as both a philosophical and literary reaction against prevailing rationalism and enlightenment dogma, advocating for revitalizing a connection with nature and rejecting materialism. Emerson employs aphorisms to elucidate how to live life to its fullest extent. His aphorism that "most of the shadows of this life are caused by standing in one’s own sunshine" contends that obstacles stem primarily from internal negativity and self-doubt, advocating for taking personal responsibility for our trajectories rather than dismissing circumstance as unfair.

The Tragedy of Julius Caesar

by William Shakespeare
Completed: Aug 2022
Rating: 9.2/10
Type: Personal Reflection
01 / Verdict

The dispute regarding the ethics behind the exaggeration of reality to constitute a better outcome for all parties remains unresolved.

— me, Aug 2022
02 / Story

As Julius Caesar returns triumphant from civil war, a faction of Roman senators led by Gaius Cassius conspires to assassinate him before he accepts a crown. Cassius recruits Marcus Brutus, a revered philosopher-patrician, convincing him that Caesar's murder is an act of patriotic duty necessary to preserve the Roman Republic.

03 / Resonance

In The Tragedy of Julius Caesar, William Shakespeare explores how the practice of manipulation is especially unseemly as it twists the victims' true perceptions of a situation. Gaius Cassius Longinus - known for his mastery of flattery and pathos - portrays his expertise while claiming that there are no such men as noble Brutus, coercing other characters into adhering to his conspiratorial desires.

04 / Reflection

Antony paints the conspirators as enemies by outlining his allegiance and dedication to Caesar to undermine their attempts to maintain public support. Antony's funeral oration ('Friends, Romans, countrymen') systematically dismantles Brutus's rational argument with theatrical emotional appeals, inciting the Roman mob into riotous rebellion. Shakespeare warns that political crowds are fundamentally fickle, easily steered by oratorical craft rather than ethical truth.

05 / Takeaway

The principal characters of the play employ both endearing and robust remarks to achieve their desired outcome: outplay their adversaries while maintaining public support. Specifically, Cassius displays his proficiency in manipulation to control Brutus's allegiance, while Antony leverages oratorical craft to incite rebellion. The dispute regarding the ethics behind the exaggeration of reality to constitute a better outcome remains unresolved, illustrating that political rhetoric often masks personal ambition behind the guise of noble duty.

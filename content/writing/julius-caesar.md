### What It's About
As Julius Caesar returns triumphant from civil war, a faction of Roman senators led by Gaius Cassius conspires to assassinate him before he accepts a crown. Cassius recruits Marcus Brutus, a revered philosopher-patrician, convincing him that Caesar's murder is an act of patriotic duty necessary to preserve the Roman Republic.

### What Resonated Most
The dispute regarding the ethics behind the exaggeration of reality to constitute a better outcome for all parties remains unresolved. In The Tragedy of Julius Caesar, William Shakespeare explores how the practice of manipulation is especially unseemly as it twists the victims' true perceptions of a situation. Gaius Cassius Longinus - known for his mastery of flattery and pathos - portrays his expertise while claiming that there are no such men as noble Brutus, coercing other characters into adhering to his conspiratorial desires.

The principal characters of the play employ both endearing and robust remarks to achieve their desired outcome: outplay their adversaries while maintaining public support. Specifically, Cassius displays his proficiency in manipulation to control Brutus's allegiance, while Antony leverages oratorical craft to incite rebellion.
